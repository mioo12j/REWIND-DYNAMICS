Company  ·  Human Rights

Human rights.

Building autonomous systems for defence carries a direct responsibility to think about how they could be used, and by whom. This sets out our position, the checks we apply, and the limits we will not move.

Why This Page Exists

The technology
makes it unavoidable.

§ 01 / CONTEXT

A company building autonomous systems cannot treat human rights as a procurement formality. The technology we develop can reduce risk to people — that is the point of it — but the same capability, misapplied or supplied to the wrong party, could do the opposite.

We would rather state our limits publicly, before commercial pressure makes them inconvenient, than discover them under it.

Our Commitments

Lines we
hold.

§ 02 / COMMITMENTS
01

A person is always in command

Machines carry out tasks; people set the rules and remain accountable. We will not build a system designed to remove a human from that role, and this is not negotiable commercially.

02

End-use and end-user assessment

Before we engage, we assess what a system would be used for and by whom, alongside sanctions and export-control screening. Any one of these can stop the engagement.

03

We decline work

Where we judge that a party is unable or unwilling to use a system lawfully and responsibly, we decline — and we accept the commercial cost of that.

04

Compliance with applicable law

We operate within international humanitarian law obligations as they apply through the jurisdictions and customers we work with.

05

Accountability by design

Our systems record what they did and why, so decisions can be reconstructed and someone can be answerable for them.

06

Labour standards

We expect fair, lawful and safe employment throughout our own operations and our supply chain.

Supply Chain

What we expect
of suppliers.

Our supply chain is currently small, which makes it practical to know who we buy from and to ask directly rather than rely on attestations alone.

  • No forced or child labourWe do not knowingly work with suppliers using forced, bonded, trafficked or child labour, at any tier we can see.
  • Lawful employmentFair wages, lawful hours and safe conditions for the people who make what we buy.
  • Willingness to answerSuppliers should expect to be asked, and to be able to answer. Reluctance to answer is itself information.
  • Cascade of expectationWe expect suppliers to hold their own suppliers to a comparable standard.
