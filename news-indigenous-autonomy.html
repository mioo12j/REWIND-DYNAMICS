The case for trustworthy autonomy in defence

Trustworthy autonomy is not a luxury — it is fast becoming a requirement. Our view on why defence forces should be able to own the systems they depend on.

Every serious defence establishment is investing in autonomy. Uncrewed and autonomous systems are changing how surveillance is done, how forces are protected, and how risk is managed. The question for any force is not whether to adopt them, but whether to own them.

The problem with importing capability

Buying finished systems from abroad can be quick, but it comes with quiet costs. You inherit someone else's design choices, you depend on their supply chain and support, and the most important parts — the autonomy and the software — often arrive as a sealed box you cannot fully inspect or adapt.

For something as consequential as autonomy, that is a poor trade. Capability you cannot understand is capability you cannot truly trust.

Owned capability is trustworthy capability

When a system is designed, built and understood in-house, it can be inspected, adapted and maintained on your own terms. It can be tuned for the conditions it actually operates in. And the expertise compounds over time instead of leaving with a vendor.

Trustworthy autonomy isn't about where a system is made. It's about being able to trust, adapt and improve the systems you depend on.

Our part in it

This is the space Rewind Dynamics is being built to work in. We are focused on autonomy — unmanned aerial, precision-guided and tactical systems — engineered from first principles and owned end to end. It is a long road, and no single company covers all of it, but a healthy field is built one serious team at a time.

We're glad to be one of them.
