Flagship Concept

Two-tier autonomy:
a mother, and its children.

§ 01 / NANO-DRONE

One of our most exciting research directions is a two-tier "mother–child" architecture for autonomous nano-drones. A larger carrier platform — the "mother" — travels to the area of interest and releases a group of very small, expendable "child" drones to do the close-in work.

The idea is a division of labour. The mother carries range, endurance, compute and communications; the children carry almost nothing but their sensors and their autonomy. Because the children are tiny, they can go where a larger aircraft can't — low, close, and quiet — while the mother stays back, coordinates them and relays what they find.

For defence, that opens up reconnaissance and observation in cluttered, confined or contested spaces without putting a person or an expensive platform forward. The children act as a forward layer of eyes; the mother is the brain and the link back to the people in command.

Concept

Autonomous surveillance & ISR.

A second direction is persistent, autonomous observation — intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance that runs with minimal operator load. The aim is simple to state: watch an area reliably, understand what's normal, and flag what isn't — so a human can decide what it means.

We're deliberately keeping this one high-level. The value for defence is a forward set of patient, discreet eyes that reduce risk to personnel and extend how much a small team can keep watch over. A person always sets the tasking and stays in command of what's done with it.

More Directions

Other problems
we're exploring.

The hard, shared problems underneath everything above. Progress on any of these lifts every platform we build.

§ 02 / OPEN QUESTIONS
01

Swarming

Many small autonomous systems acting as one coordinated team — sharing what they see, splitting the task, and staying useful even if some are lost.

02

GPS-denied navigation

Knowing where you are and finding your way when satellite signals are jammed, spoofed or simply unavailable — using what the system can see and sense.

03

Onboard perception

Making sense of a cluttered, changing world in real time, on the platform itself — the difference between reacting and truly understanding.

04

Edge intelligence

Running capable autonomy on tiny, power-limited hardware, so a small system stays effective without a constant link home.

05

Trustworthy autonomy

Autonomy that stays predictable, safe and reviewable under stress — behaviour a commander can rely on and account for afterwards.

06

Human–machine teaming

The right hand-offs between operator and system, so autonomy amplifies a person's judgement instead of replacing it.

Not simply a lot of drones, but a group of autonomous systems that coordinate as one team — sharing what each sees, dividing a task between them, and continuing to function when individual members are lost. That last property, graceful attrition, is what makes the idea powerful: losing one member degrades the group slightly rather than ending the task.
