Application 01  ·  ISR

Intelligence,
surveillance,
reconnaissance.

ISR is the work of finding out what is happening somewhere, so that decisions can be made on fact rather than assumption. It is the single largest and most immediate application of autonomy in defence — and the one where the gap between what is needed and what is affordable is widest.

The Problem

Watching is expensive.

§ 01 / PROBLEM

Surveillance is fundamentally a problem of attention. Watching an area properly means watching it continuously, and human attention degrades badly over long, uneventful periods — which is precisely what good surveillance consists of. Staffing it properly is expensive; staffing it improperly is worse than not doing it.

Reconnaissance has a different cost: to answer a question about a place, someone usually has to go there. That exposure is often the most dangerous part of an operation, and it is undertaken to obtain information rather than to achieve anything at the location itself.

How Autonomy Changes It

Persistent watch,
without persistent staffing.

An autonomous system does not get bored. It can hold a pattern for as long as its endurance allows, notice what changes, and escalate only what merits a person's attention.

  • PersistenceSustained observation over long periods, at a cost that makes continuous coverage realistic.
  • Reconnaissance without exposureSending a machine to answer the question instead of a person.
  • Conclusions, not raw feedReporting what changed and why it might matter, rather than hours of video for someone to review.
  • Attention where it countsOperators supervising several systems and spending their judgement on the exceptions.

What It Demands

Why ISR is a
hard autonomy problem.

§ 02 / DEMANDS
01

Knowing what 'normal' is

Flagging change requires a model of the baseline — which the system has to build for itself.

02

Suppressing false alarms

An ISR system that reports constantly is quickly ignored, which makes precision more valuable than sensitivity.

03

Distance and scale

The subjects of interest are usually far away and small in the frame.

04

Endurance

Persistence is an energy problem before it is a software problem.

05

Bandwidth discipline

There is rarely enough link to send everything, so the system must decide what is worth sending.

06

Auditability

Anything that informs a decision has to be reviewable afterwards.

Common Questions

Questions about
autonomous ISR.

§ 98 / FAQ
ISR stands for intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance — the work of gathering information about an area or situation so that commanders can decide on fact rather than assumption. Surveillance is watching an area persistently over time; reconnaissance is going to answer a specific question about a specific place.
An autonomous system does not lose attention over long, uneventful periods the way a human operator does — and vigilance research shows human attention on a low-event task degrades within tens of minutes. Autonomy lets a platform hold a pattern for as long as its endurance allows, learn what the area's normal state looks like, and escalate only what merits a person's attention. The operator spends their judgement on exceptions rather than on watching a feed.
A surveillance system that raises the alarm without cause trains its operators to ignore it, and an ignored system provides no coverage regardless of how good its detection looks on paper. So precision — how much of what it reports is worth reporting — matters more than sensitivity, which is not the conventional emphasis but is the behaviourally correct one.
Persistent surveillance is sustained observation of an area over long periods, at a cost that makes continuous coverage realistic. It is fundamentally an endurance and attention problem before it is a sensing problem: the limiting factor was rarely how long something could stay aloft, but how much human attention its output consumed.
