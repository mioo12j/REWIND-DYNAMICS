Preparing for our first mission test this December

Everything we have built so far has lived in development and simulation. This December we plan to put it to its first real-world mission test.

We want to be plain about where we are. In six years, Rewind Dynamics has not fielded, deployed or proven an autonomous system in the real world. Everything we have built so far lives in development and in simulation. That has been deliberate.

Why we've taken our time

Autonomy for defence is not a place to cut corners. We have spent our time on the fundamentals — the autonomy core, perception and the systems engineering underneath — and proving behaviour in software, where a difficult scenario can be repeated a thousand times at no risk. We would rather earn confidence slowly than claim it early.

What the first mission test is

This December, for the first time, we plan to take that work out of simulation and into a first end-to-end mission test — an autonomous system attempting a representative task in the real world, start to finish, under our supervision.

We're calling it what it is: a test. The point is not a polished demonstration; it is to find out honestly what holds up outside the model and what doesn't. Some of it will work. Some of it will send us back to the bench. Both are the job.

We don't test to prove we're right. We test to find out where we're wrong — while it's still cheap to fix.

How we'll go about it

The approach is the one that has guided us from the start: software-proven first, a human firmly in command throughout, and an honest assessment afterward of what went right, what went wrong, and why. Whatever the outcome, we'll learn from it and feed it straight back into the next iteration.

Where this leads

A first mission test is a beginning, not a finish line. It marks the point where our research starts meeting reality — and it's exactly the kind of milestone that partners, evaluators and investors tend to want to follow closely. If that's you, we'd be glad to talk: please get in touch.
