Why the tactical edge belongs to autonomy

Small, autonomous systems change what a dismounted team can see and do. Our view on why the tactical edge is where autonomy earns its keep.

Big platforms get the attention, but some of the most useful autonomy is small enough to carry. Compact autonomous systems — the kind a soldier can put in a pack — quietly change what a dismounted team can see and do.

Extending the senses of a small team

A tactical autonomous system can look around the next corner, watch a route, or hold an eye on an area — without putting a person in the exposed position to do it. That's a meaningful shift in risk and awareness for the people at the sharp end.

The discipline of small

Working at this scale is its own engineering discipline. Size, weight and power are all tightly limited, so every capability has to earn its place. Simplicity and ruggedness matter more than features; a system that's fiddly or fragile is worse than no system at all.

At the tactical edge, dependable and simple beats clever and delicate every time.

Where we fit

Tactical miniature systems are one of the three families we develop, precisely because the constraints are so demanding. Getting capable autonomy into a small, rugged, man-portable package is exactly the kind of hard problem we started Rewind Dynamics to work on.
