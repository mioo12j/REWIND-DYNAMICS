In Development

What we're
building.

We are developing three families of autonomous systems for defence applications. Each is at an early stage — the descriptions below explain what the category is and the kind of work it involves, not a finished product.

These are areas of active development and research. We are not publishing specifications or performance figures, and nothing here should be read as a claim of a fielded or completed system. For anything specific, please get in touch.

System 01

Unmanned Aerial Vehicles

Aircraft that fly without a pilot on board. Our interest is in autonomous UAVs for observation and defence tasks — where the aircraft can navigate and carry out a task with limited human input.

UAVs bring together flight control, navigation, perception and communications. The engineering challenge is making them dependable and useful in real conditions — which is exactly the kind of work we're focused on.

System 02

Humanoid Systems

Robots built in a human-like form — with a body and limbs designed to work in spaces and with tools made for people. This is a research area for us, focused on mobility, balance and control.

Humanoid robotics is one of the hardest problems in the field, drawing on control theory, perception and machine learning. We're studying where a human-scale platform genuinely makes sense for defence tasks.

System 03

Tactical Miniature Systems

Small autonomous systems built for close-range, constrained environments — where size, weight and power are tightly limited and the system has to be simple, rugged and reliable.

Working at small scale is its own discipline: fitting capable autonomy and sensing onto compact, low-power hardware. It's an area we find especially interesting for defence use.
